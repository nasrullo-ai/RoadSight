Findings that changed the design

  • Box bottoms are unreliable in dense traffic. In the shallow-angle city clip, closer vehicles cover the bottom of farther ones, and the foot point jumps. Velocity is now taken from the box top, which is rarely covered; the ground point is the top plus the track's median box height.
  • Boxes cut by the frame border lie. A car entering from the bottom edge appeared to drive backwards because its box bottom was pinned to the border. Border rows are excluded from kinematics.
  • Tracks jump between vehicles. After two-second occlusions ByteTrack sometimes re-attached an ID to another car across the frame. Tracks are now split at jumps faster than 20 body lengths per second.
  • Noise differs per video. The 1-in-100 braking value in normal traffic is 1.3 BL/s² on the highway and 6.4 BL/s² in the city clip. Near-miss and risk thresholds therefore scale with each video's own noise.
  • Most missed vehicles were faint, not invisible. At 640 px the detector found 22 boxes in a busy city frame, but only 5 were confident enough to start a track. Detecting at 960 px and starting tracks at 0.3 raised coverage from 4.5 to 23.8 tracked objects per frame.
  • Lanes can be learned. Two directions of travel separate cleanly in the flow field, which lets wrong-way detection run before anyone draws lanes.
