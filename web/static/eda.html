Findings that changed the design

From the four organizer samples: one signalised Tashkent intersection, 4K at 29.97 fps, 10-bit XAVC, two clips at noon and two at dusk (one close to night).

  • It is a busy junction, not a highway. 22 to 25 cars and 17 to 26 pedestrians are in view at any moment; each five-minute clip has 360 to 430 car tracks and 500 to 830 pedestrian tracks. Pedestrian classes (jaywalking, failure to yield) therefore matter as much as vehicle classes, and riders and passengers must be told apart from pedestrians.
  • The signal decides most events. Two signal heads are in view. The main approach sees reds of about 40 s and greens of 7 to 20 s, so most vehicles in view are queued. Waiting in that queue is not a stopped vehicle and not congestion, which is why both rules exclude learned signal-queue cells and red phases.
  • Green ends with a flashing phase. The detected green splits into many short pieces separated by "unknown" frames, consistent with the flashing green that ends each phase here. The signal state is therefore smoothed over 1 s, and red-light running is only reported after a 0.5 s grace period on a steady red.
  • 4K decoding is the bottleneck, not the detector. Decoding every frame costs more than detecting on a sample of them. Part A lets ffmpeg skip non-reference frames and downscale to 1920 px, while the signal heads are cut out at full resolution so their lamps stay sharp.
  • The camera moves between recordings. Against the frame the scene was drawn on, the clips are shifted by 2 to 105 pixels (of 3840), enough to put a stop line on the wrong side of a car. The scene is drawn once and aligned to every video by SIFT and a homography (280 inlier matches at dusk, up to 3,000 at noon); if alignment fails, the drawn lines are dropped rather than trusted.
  • Queues spill into the junction at noon. Vehicles wait past the stop line and inside the box, which is exactly where stop-line violations happen, and also why the junction box is excluded from wrong-way checks (turning cars cross the opposite lanes there).

Lessons from the development clips

  • Box bottoms are unreliable in dense traffic. Closer vehicles cover the bottom of farther ones, so velocity is taken from the box top; the ground point is the top plus the track's median box height.
  • Boxes cut by the frame border lie. Border rows are excluded from kinematics.
  • Tracks jump between vehicles after occlusions. Tracks are split at jumps faster than 20 body lengths per second.
  • Most missed vehicles were faint, not invisible. Detecting at 960 px and starting tracks at 0.3 raised coverage from 4.5 to 23.8 tracked objects per frame on a busy city clip.
